Negotiation Strategy

A consolidated analysis of how I prepare for negotiations, evaluate proposals, and work with parties who have different priorities. Selected classroom exercises provide examples of the approach.

My contribution
Preparation, participation, and analysis
Tools
Priority matrix, stakeholder analysis, and peer feedback
Focus
Decision quality and coordinated bargaining
Setting
Classroom negotiation simulations
01

Preparation and proposal evaluation

My preparation separates priorities, possible outcomes, and the alternative to an agreement. In the Baxter / Local 190 exercise, I used a matrix to compare operating, compensation, and employment terms. This made it easier to evaluate a package of proposals and identify what remained unclear.

Negotiation planning framework

DecisionPreparationUse
PrioritiesRank essential and flexible termsIdentify where to hold firm or trade
ProposalsCompare the full packageAssess concessions alongside what is received
AlternativesConsider the outcome without agreementReduce pressure to accept unsuitable terms
ApprovalMap decision makers and potential alliesCoordinate support for an agreement
02

Stakeholder alignment

In multi-party negotiations, the internal discussion can be as important as the offer exchanged between sides. I considered who shared an objective, whose approval mattered, and how individual decisions could affect the group’s bargaining position.

03

Concessions and communication

Across the exercises, I examined how to keep a constructive tone without giving away important terms. My reflections emphasized asking what a concession achieves, seeking reciprocal movement, and using questions to understand the other side before revising an offer.

04

Practical takeaways

The work strengthened my preparation process: define the limits, understand the alternative, identify who can approve a deal, and compare the complete proposal. Peer feedback also helped me recognize when collaboration supported the outcome and when a firmer position was needed.
