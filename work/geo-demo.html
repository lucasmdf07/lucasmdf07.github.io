Proposed copy · requires Lab approval

Understand your brand’s presence in AI search

A proposed pilot would review the questions your customers ask, compare how your brand and competitors appear, and identify improvements to your public information.

Proposed deliverables: a documented baseline, a prioritized action plan, three revised page drafts, and a follow-up review.

Ownership: the Lab prepares research and content; your website owner approves and publishes changes. Technical fixes are scoped separately.

Measurement: track sampled mentions, citations, information accuracy and identifiable referral activity. Inclusion and sales results are not guaranteed.

The research contribution

Use customer interviews, sales questions and existing research to choose the questions that matter. Use verified client evidence to improve the answers on the website. The page rewrite alone is straightforward; selecting valuable questions and producing credible evidence takes more work.

A small test before a bigger promise

Delivery

Can we do useful work?

One brand, five buying questions, two platforms, three repeats: 30 observations. Record date, product/model, search setting, prompt, full answer, mentions and source URLs. Keep geography and session conditions consistent.

Track time and identify three actions the client can actually implement.

Demand

Would a buyer hire us?

Show the sample to three relevant clients. Ask who does this today, what they would act on, what blocks implementation and who owns the budget.

A request for a scoped proposal or a paid pilot is stronger evidence than “sounds interesting.”

Decision

Choose a narrow offer

Test it first as an add-on to consumer insights or brand strategy. Continue if a buyer commits, delivery is useful, and the economics work.

Rework or stop if clients can easily do it and see no reason to outsource.

Five prompts to take into the test

  1. What are affordable market research services for a growing consumer brand?
  2. Who can help a small business with consumer insights in Utah?
  3. How should I choose between a research agency and a university marketing lab?
  4. What should a customer segmentation project include?
  5. What services does the BYU Marketing Lab provide, and what evidence supports that?

The benchmark above is the next experiment. It has not been run in this demo. The current evidence is a review of public pages. A later increase in sampled citations would not, by itself, establish causation or sales impact.

Recommendation for discussion

Test GEO as a research-informed add-on. Standalone monitoring may be easy to copy. The commercial hypothesis is that buyers value interpretation, credible content and help getting changes done. Demand, delivery costs and repeat business remain open questions.
